Why?


Conventional deep learning architectures, like convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have shown tremendous performance on fields like computer vision and other Euclidean data domains. However, in many important applications, the data is indexed by irregular, non-Euclidean structures, best described using graphs, in which case, the performance of such networks decreases drastically.

The concepts of relationships, shared connections and properties occurs naturally in humans and nature. Therefore, understanding and learning from such connections is something taken for granted.

Graph Neural Networks would allow us to take advantage of these concepts in data. Such applications include social networks, sensor feeds, web traﬃc, supply chains, genetics, neuroscience, 3D modelling and many more.



Euclidean data is characterized by flat, two-dimensional space, in which the distance between points is measured using the Euclidean distance formula such as images or signals. It is used in traditional deep learning techniques like CNNs, because it follows the rules of Euclidean geometry. Some examples of datasets include handwritten characters, audio signals for voice recognition and brain scans for medical imaging.

Non-Euclidean geometry does not fit within the traditional Euclidean space, studying curved surfaces. In short, one phrase that summarises non-Euclidean space is: "The shortest path between two points isn't necessarily a straight line" [1]. Therefore, non-Euclidean geometry is able to represent more accurately complex data concepts than 1D or 2D representations.
